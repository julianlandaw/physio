Single-compartment respiratory mechanics

Pressure, flow, resistance, and compliance — all on one page.

This module treats the respiratory system as a compliant compartment connected to a resistive airway. It is intentionally simple, but it captures the core bedside relationships that distinguish volume-control and pressure-control ventilation.

Key variables

Paw: airway pressurePEEP: baseline pressureV: volume above FRCV̇: flowR: resistanceC: compliance

The simulators show airway pressure, flow, and volume over time and report inspired/expired tidal volume, peak pressure, plateau pressure, mean airway pressure, and intrinsic PEEP.

The model

The elementary equation of motion for a passive lung is:

Paw = PEEP + V/C + R · V̇

The pressure measured at the airway opening is the sum of three parts:

  • Baseline pressure, PEEP.
  • Elastic pressure, V/C, which rises as the lung fills and is larger when compliance is low.
  • Resistive pressure, R · V̇, which appears only when flow is present and grows with airway resistance.

During a pause/hold, flow is zero, so the resistive term disappears. That is why plateau pressure estimates elastic recoil pressure, while peak inspiratory pressure includes both elastic and resistive components.

Time constant

The product τ = R · C determines how quickly the compartment fills or empties after a pressure step.

V(t) → C · ΔP with time constant τ = R · C

After roughly 3 time constants, about 95% of a pressure-driven volume change has occurred. Short expiratory time or a long time constant can leave residual volume at end-expiration, producing intrinsic PEEP.

Volume-control ventilation

In volume control, the ventilator targets a set tidal volume. In this teaching model, inspiration is delivered with constant flow, optionally followed by an inspiratory hold.

V̇insp = VT / Tflow

Because flow is prescribed, volume increases linearly during the flow phase. Airway pressure is whatever pressure is required to push that flow through the resistance and stretch the lung:

Paw = PEEP + V/C + R · V̇insp

Teaching point: Raising resistance increases peak pressure without changing plateau pressure much; lowering compliance raises both peak and plateau pressure.

Pressure-control ventilation

In pressure control, the ventilator targets an inspiratory pressure. Flow is not set directly; it decelerates as alveolar pressure approaches the applied airway pressure.

dV/dt = (Pdrive − V/C) / R

The delivered tidal volume depends on driving pressure, inspiratory time, resistance, compliance, and how much residual volume remains at the start of inspiration.

Teaching point: Increasing inspiratory pressure or compliance increases tidal volume; increasing resistance can reduce delivered volume if inspiratory time is too short.

How to use the simulators

  1. Start with normal values: RR 12/min, I:E around 1:2, PEEP 5 cm H₂O, compliance 50 mL/cm H₂O, resistance 10 cm H₂O/L/s.
  2. For obstructive physiology, increase resistance and shorten expiratory time to see air trapping and intrinsic PEEP.
  3. For restrictive physiology, reduce compliance and observe the rise in plateau pressure for a given volume, or the fall in delivered volume for a given pressure.

These are educational models, not clinical ventilator calculators. Real ventilators, leaks, patient effort, nonlinear mechanics, recruitment, and humidified gas dynamics are not modeled.
